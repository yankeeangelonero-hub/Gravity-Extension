The hatch seal broke with a hiss that sounded too loud for 0340.

Mu was still in the briefing room when Autumn heard it — Flay Allster's voice carrying from the corridor like she wanted witnesses. Three off-duty crew behind her, arms crossed, none of them doing anything useful like sleeping.

"—you don't get to play hero with a Coordinator on a leash and call it mercy."

Autumn stepped out. Didn't close the hatch behind her. Let Mu hear it if he wanted to.

Flay was seventeen, maybe eighteen. Red hair pulled back hard enough to change her face shape. Earth Alliance uniform worn like a statement — every crease deliberate, the kind of perfection that says I belong here and you don't. Her father's daughter. George Allster's money had probably bought half the Archangel's medical supplies.

"She's a POW under Geneva protocols. Not a pet."

"Protocols." Flay's mouth twisted. "Is that what they taught you in Heliopolis? Before Kira left us to die?"

The corridor went quiet. The three crew members suddenly found the bulkhead rivets interesting.

Autumn kept her hands still. Didn't lean forward, didn't step back. Gave Flay exactly as much ground as a wall gives — none, but nothing to push against either.

"Kira made his choice. I'm making mine. You can disagree with it in the morning when the watch commander's office is open."

Flay's eyes narrowed. She was looking for the crack — the tell that said this Natural girl from the colonies was scared, or guilty, or hiding something. She didn't find it.

What she couldn't know was what Mu could see from the briefing room doorway: Autumn's hands hadn't moved. Not a twitch. Not a micro-correction. The kind of stillness that fighter pilots train for years to achieve and most never do.

Mu filed that away.

Two decks below, in the medical bay, Lacus Clyne sat perfectly still on her cot. The ventilation carried sound in this ship — a design flaw the engineers knew about and hadn't fixed because they'd never expected to carry prisoners. She couldn't make out every word, but she heard "Coordinator sympathizer" clearly enough.

She heard the crew go quiet.

She heard a young woman's voice — not the angry one — respond with something measured and calm. The kind of calm that costs something to maintain.

Lacus added a line to the mental ledger she was building. The pilot. Politically exposed. Crew hostility aimed at her for showing basic decency. That meant the pilot was either principled or performing — and either way, she was burning social capital that a Natural on this ship couldn't afford to waste.

Useful. Potentially an ally. Definitely a pressure point others would exploit.

Lacus smoothed the thin blanket over her knees and waited for morning.
