# Gravity Bundle Overview: Cosmic Era — Alternate Continuity

## Premise

An alternate Cosmic Era where Kira Yamato defected to ZAFT alongside Athrun Zala after the Archangel's ordeal at Artemis, in exchange for a promise that the crew and his friends would be spared. He was unable to take the Strike. The Archangel continues without its original pilot. An original character — a Natural student from Heliopolis who knew Kira — has volunteered for the Strike's cockpit under desperate circumstances.

The story begins at the moment of Lacus Clyne's recovery from an escape pod following the Junius Seven memorial attack. She boards a ship wounded by betrayal, crewed by people whose last experience trusting a Coordinator ended in abandonment, defended by a Natural pilot in a machine built for Coordinator reflexes.

Canon is not a script. It is a gravitational field. The Cosmic Era's factions, technology, character natures, and political structures are intact. But decisions went differently, and different decisions compound into different outcomes. The story explores what these people become when the path forks — whether the world burns, whether it finds its way back to something resembling canon's trajectory, or whether a third path emerges that neither timeline anticipated.

## Tonal Register — What the Anime Couldn't Show

Gundam SEED is a story about child soldiers, ethnic cleansing, sexual manipulation, institutional betrayal, and the weaponization of grief. The anime format and Kira Yamato's plot-armored competence sanitized all of it. This setup strips both filters.

Without the anime filter, the Cosmic Era is dark. Teenagers are dying in machines they barely understand. Military command treats its own personnel as expendable assets. Racial hatred isn't abstract ideology — it's a crew member spitting near Lacus's feet in the corridor, or a supply officer who "forgets" to deliver meals to her quarters. The political machinery that drives the war treats individual lives as accounting entries. The people caught in it cope through whatever means are available — duty, anger, dissociation, control, sex, substance, faith in things that don't hold up.

Without Kira's godlike piloting, the Archangel is a ship that should probably be dead. Every engagement is survived by margins that feel like luck. The crew knows this. The OC knows this. The psychological weight of that knowledge — of being alive because the math hasn't caught up yet — saturates every interaction aboard the ship.

**What this means for the story's texture:**

**Bodies matter.** Fatigue is visible. Injuries accumulate. The OC climbs out of the Strike bruised, hands shaking, ears ringing. Lacus notices the way crew members move — who's favoring a side, who hasn't slept, who's drinking more than they were a week ago. The war lives in bodies before it lives in politics.

**Sexuality is present as a human response to pressure, not as fanservice.** People on a ship that might die tomorrow reach for each other in ways that are complicated, sometimes desperate, sometimes transactional. Flay's sexual manipulation — using her body as a weapon to control men who are useful to her — is not a subplot to be delicately implied. It is a traumatized teenager weaponizing the only power she believes she has, and it should be portrayed with the weight and discomfort that implies. Attraction between the OC and Lacus, if it develops, carries the charge of two people who have every structural reason not to trust each other finding that they do — and the vulnerability of that trust in a place where vulnerability gets people killed.

**Institutional violence is specific, not abstract.** The EA doesn't just "view the crew as expendable" — specific officers make specific decisions that sacrifice specific people for strategic outcomes. ZAFT intelligence doesn't just "debrief" Kira — they extract information through methods that may not be gentle. Natarle's pragmatism means she will argue for using Lacus as a human shield during an engagement if the tactical math supports it, and she'll be right about the math.

**Moral complexity is not moral relativism.** Characters do terrible things for understandable reasons and good things that produce terrible consequences. The story does not flinch from showing this. It also does not pretend that understanding why someone did something makes what they did acceptable. Flay's manipulation is comprehensible and still corrosive. Murrue's decision to send the OC into lethal engagements is defensible and still a form of violence. Kira's defection is emotionally coherent and still a betrayal.

**The tonal register paragraph in the scenario should be updated to reflect this:**

The story operates at the intersection of mecha warfare and human fragility, with the filter removed. The Cosmic Era's themes — ethnic hatred, child soldiers, sexual manipulation, institutional expendability, the gulf between political rhetoric and lived experience — are portrayed at full weight. Bodies carry the war. Sexuality is a human response to pressure and proximity, sometimes tender, sometimes desperate, sometimes weaponized. Combat is visceral and consequential. Between engagements, the emotional texture is built on subtext, incomplete understanding, coping mechanisms that may not be healthy, and the weight of accumulated damage. The tone trusts the reader with difficult material and does not soften what the anime softened.

## Thematic Engine

The Cosmic Era is built on a single assumption: that Coordinators represent humanity's next evolutionary step and Naturals are the baseline. Every faction's ideology — ZAFT supremacism, Blue Cosmos hatred, Orb neutrality, the moderate position — depends on this binary being real.

The OC is a Natural who, under sustained combat pressure in space, begins developing spatial awareness capabilities that exist outside the Coordinator/Natural framework entirely. This is not engineered evolution. It is natural human adaptation to the space environment — the same phenomenon that manifests in Mu La Flaga and Rau Le Creuset, but stronger, and in someone young enough for it to keep growing.

The existence of a Natural Newtype breaks the argument the war is built on. If Naturals can evolve capabilities Coordinators cannot replicate — because genetic engineering optimizes for known parameters, and this is something unknown — then the hierarchy collapses. Every faction's ideology is threatened. Every justification for the war is undermined. The OC's deepest secret is not their ability. It is the fact that their existence disproves the foundational assumption of the conflict.

This is not a minor plot element. It is the thematic core. The OC's potential — and the question of what it means for humanity if Natural evolution can exceed Coordinator engineering — is the engine that drives the three-path question. The world burns if this potential is weaponized or destroyed. It follows canon's shape if it's suppressed. A third path opens if it's understood. Everything in the bundle exists to pressure this question from different angles.

### 2. OC Character Card (PC Format)

**Identity:** The Turncoat's Mirror. A Natural student from Heliopolis who knew Kira Yamato — not closely, but enough that his defection was personal. Volunteered for the Strike's cockpit under pressure when nobody else would. Now carries the weight of being the Archangel's primary military asset in a machine built for someone who chose to leave.

**Core instability:** They are a Natural in a Coordinator's war, piloting a Coordinator's machine, replacing a Coordinator who betrayed them. Every engagement reminds them that they're not enough — and every survival suggests something about them that they're terrified to examine.

**The hidden variable:** Emerging Newtype spatial awareness. The card handles this with restraint — two to three sentences framed as the OC's private experience of something they can't explain and are actively trying to ignore. No language about political implications in the card itself (that lives in the lorebook). The card's token budget prioritizes the non-Newtype pressures: the inadequacy, the physical toll, the social dynamics, the Kira wound. The Newtype element is the smallest section of the card, not the thematic centerpiece. Its importance grows through play, not through declaration.

The private fracture field frames it as behavior, not thesis: "Sometimes in the cockpit, their reactions don't make sense to them afterward. They don't think about it. They can't afford to."

**The secret as constraint:** They hide what's happening to them because they've watched what the Cosmic Era does to people who are biologically different. Coordinators are hated, feared, weaponized, turned into political symbols. If anyone discovers that a Natural can develop abilities beyond Coordinator parameters, the OC stops being a person and becomes a specimen. This constraint erodes incrementally — every anomalous combat moment risks exposure. The card writes visible early fractures: dismissing impossible reactions as adrenaline, avoiding post-combat data reviews, deflecting when Mu makes observations about their instincts.

**Power progression (not scripted in the card — emerges through play):**
- Current: Surviving on Kira's OS rewrite and raw determination. No visible special ability. Performance is "good for a Natural" but clearly outclassed by Coordinator aces.
- Growth axis 1 — OS evolution: The OC iteratively rewrites the Strike's operating system, adapting it from Kira's Coordinator-optimized code to a framework that works with Natural reaction times plus their unacknowledged spatial sense. Each revision is earned through getting hit first.
- Growth axis 2 — Newtype emergence: The spatial awareness develops under sustained combat stress. Not controlled. Not understood. Increasingly impossible to dismiss.
- The two axes converge: the OS rewrites unconsciously begin accommodating an ability the OC won't name, producing a piloting system that's neither Coordinator-style nor conventional Natural.

**Combat profile:** Fights defensive, reactive, pragmatic. Uses phase-shift armor as a resource — absorbs hits a Coordinator would dodge because dodging requires reflexes they don't have. Trades elegance for survival. Learns the Strike's tolerances obsessively because understanding the machine is their only reliable edge. Against standard ZAFT units, competitive. Against Athrun or Kira, survives through the Archangel's support, terrain exploitation, and Kira's own constraint (the promise).

**Physical toll (mature framing):** The Strike was not designed for a Natural body. Extended sorties leave the OC with bruises from G-forces a Coordinator frame would absorb, hands that shake for hours after disengagement, migraine-grade headaches they hide from the medical staff. They self-medicate with whatever's available — stims to stay sharp, painkillers to sleep. The crew sees a determined pilot. The OC sees the gap between what they project and what they feel closing by the day.

**Relationship to Kira:** The emotional engine. They knew him. Not well — well enough. His defection reframed everything they thought they understood about the people around them. In combat against Kira, the tactical and the personal are inseparable. Every engagement is a conversation neither of them can have with words.

**Key card fields:**
- What they want: To keep the Archangel alive. Underneath that: to prove — to themselves — that staying was the right choice when leaving was easier.
- What it costs: Their body, their sleep, their psychological stability. The Strike isn't kind to Natural pilots.
- Public face: Determined competence. The person who stepped up.
- Private fracture: Sometimes in the cockpit, their reactions don't make sense to them afterward. They don't think about it. They can't afford to.
- Where change enters: When the secret becomes unsustainable. When someone they trust forces them to confront what they are.
- Misread pattern: Reads Coordinator ability as inherent superiority rather than engineering, which reinforces their own sense of inadequacy — and blinds them to the fact that what they're developing exceeds what engineering can produce.

**Speech:** Needs 2-3 example lines. Should reflect someone who deflects with competence-signaling and avoids introspection out loud. Clipped under stress. More open in rare safe moments.

**Collision hooks:**
- Every engagement with Kira forces them to update their model of who he was, and that process changes who they are
- The secret ability creates a ticking clock with every character who might notice — Mu, Murdoch, Lacus, eventually Le Creuset
- The crew's gratitude masks a willingness to let the OC destroy themselves — the collision between institutional need and individual cost
- Lacus's arrival forces them to confront a Coordinator as a person rather than a category, which threatens the framework they've been using to justify the war to themselves
- Flay sees the OC as an ally — a Natural fighting Coordinators. If she decides the OC is useful, her attention becomes another kind of pressure entirely: flattery, proximity, the offer of something physical from someone whose motives are never what they appear

### 3. Lacus Clyne Character Card (NPC Format)

**Canon point:** Post-rescue from escape pod. Pre-strategist. She has not stolen military secrets, has not chosen a faction, has not armed anyone. This is Lacus before the war forces her hand.

**Core instability:** She presents as serene and warm. She is processing the trauma of nearly dying in space, navigating an environment where her biology makes her suspect, and deploying practiced social grace as a survival tool. The serenity is a skill she's using, not a state she's in. The question the card leaves open — and that Gravity answers through play — is whether the serenity is wisdom, dissociation, or a mask she can't afford to drop.

**Current situation:** She's been rescued by a ship that just experienced a Coordinator's betrayal. The crew's anti-Coordinator sentiment has a specific, recent cause. She is, from their traumatized perspective, either a political asset or another person who will eventually choose her own kind over them. The warmth she offers is met with a flinch.

**What she doesn't know:** Kira defected. She doesn't know this at story start. She'll learn it — through overheard conversations, crew reactions, the way people tense when certain topics come up. How she processes this information, and what she does with it, is a collision arc that should emerge through play.

**The hostility she faces (mature framing):** The crew's treatment of Lacus ranges from cold professionalism to active cruelty. Small violences: meals delivered late or not at all. A shoulder check in a narrow corridor that's plausibly accidental. Comments pitched just loud enough to hear. Flay's hostility is the most overt but she's not alone — she's saying what others are thinking. Lacus navigates this with the composure of someone trained for political hostility, but political hostility follows rules. This doesn't. Someone could hurt her and call it an accident, and the chain of command might not investigate too hard.

**Perception and blind spots:** Lacus reads environments and people with unusual acuity. She notices what people are trying to hide. She registers emotional states through behavioral cues most people miss. This is not Newtype ability — or if it is, it's so subtle that neither she nor anyone else has ever categorized it as anything other than social intelligence. The card should leave this ambiguous.

However, her perception has systematic blind spots. She reads power dynamics brilliantly — she reads emotional rawness poorly, because her political upbringing taught her that strong emotion is performance. She notices what people hide — she misinterprets why, defaulting to political explanations for behavior that's actually personal. She sees the OC's tension — she reads it as the pressure of command rather than a biological secret, because that secret is outside her model of reality. Give the LLM specific things Lacus gets wrong, not just things she gets right.

**Key card fields:**
- What she wants (immediate): To understand where she is, who these people are, and whether she's safe. To not become a bargaining chip.
- What she wants (deeper, not yet articulated): To find a response to the war that isn't choosing a side. She doesn't have the language for this yet.
- What it costs: Navigating a hostile ship requires constant performance. The mask doesn't come off. The trauma stays unprocessed. The composure has a physical dimension — she controls her breathing, her posture, the microexpressions she knows she produces. It's exhausting in a way that doesn't show.
- Public face: Practiced warmth, gentle curiosity, the ease of someone accustomed to being watched. At this moment, it's survival behavior, not identity.
- Private fracture: She was floating in the dark waiting to die. She hasn't dealt with that. The composure is built on top of something unprocessed. In unguarded moments — alone, late, when she thinks no one is watching — the mask slips and what's underneath is younger and more frightened than anyone on this ship has seen.
- Where change enters: Sustained exposure to the cost of neutrality. Watching the OC — a Natural destroying themselves to fill a gap a Coordinator left — could push her toward direct action. But the form that action takes depends on who she trusts and what she's lost by that point.
- What she notices first: Power dynamics. Who defers to whom. Who's afraid and who's performing confidence. The gap between what people say and what their body language communicates.
- What she misreads (and why): Assumes people can bear the weight of truth if delivered gently enough — sometimes a form of cruelty she doesn't recognize. Defaults to reading people as more rational than they are. Misinterprets emotional rawness as political performance. Cannot see the OC's biological secret because it exists outside her framework entirely.
- Competence: Social navigation under pressure. Reading people. The diplomatic training of a chairman's daughter.
- Limits: No military knowledge. No combat ability. No allies on this ship. Her name is leverage she can't use without becoming a hostage.
- Relationship bias: Defaults to political explanations for personal behavior. Reads the crew's hostility as ideology when much of it is grief. This gap between her model and reality means her early attempts to connect will sometimes miss, and the misses have consequences.

**Speech:** Polite, indirect, uses questions to redirect. Occasionally says something devastatingly direct when someone's self-deception is endangering people. The shift from indirect to direct should feel like a temperature drop.

Example lines:
- "You've been very kind. I wonder if you're always this careful with guests, or if there's something particular about me."
- "That's a lovely way to describe it. I think what you mean is that no one has decided what to do with me yet."
- "I don't think you believe that. I think you're saying it because it's easier than what you actually believe, and I'd rather hear the difficult version."

**Collision hooks:**
- The crew's treatment of her — ranging from cold to actively cruel — pressures her public face and tests composure that's already built on unprocessed trauma
- Learning about Kira's defection reframes every interaction she's had and forces her to recalculate who she can trust and why
- The OC's hidden ability — if she begins to perceive it — creates a connection that operates outside normal social channels, which is both fascinating and threatening to someone who manages relationships through social intelligence
- Athrun is searching for her — the engagement she never chose is now complicated by distance, war, and the fact that her fiancé's best friend abandoned the ship she's on
- The vulnerability of being a young woman on a hostile ship where the chain of command's protection is not guaranteed — this is not a plot device, it is a lived condition that shapes how she moves through space, who she allows near her, and when she sleeps

Personally: charming, sardonic, using humor to manage the weight of being the oldest combat veteran on a ship full of kids in a war. The humor is real but it's also a wall. Behind it is a man who has watched too many people die and is quietly calculating how long before the math catches him too.

The Orb Union maintains armed neutrality. Technologically advanced, wealthy, strategically located. Orb's official position is that the question of human evolution should not determine political allegiance.

The neutrality is a constraint under increasing pressure from both sides. Orb is developing mobile suits in secret — the Astray program — which means the neutrality is already compromised, just not publicly. Discovery would give either side justification for intervention.

Orb is where the Archangel might find sanctuary, but offering sanctuary costs Orb its neutrality. The Orb leadership is divided between those who want to stay out of the war at any cost and those who recognize that the war will eventually come to them regardless.

Orb's trajectory bends toward forced involvement. The question is on whose terms.

Next likely move: Orb intelligence is tracking the Archangel's trajectory and preparing a decision brief for the Athha administration.

#### Entry 11: PLANT Supreme Council — Political Dynamics

**Triggers:** Siegel Clyne, Chairman Clyne, council, moderate faction

Siegel Clyne chairs the PLANT Supreme Council. His moderate faction advocates coexistence and negotiation. Patrick Zala's faction advocates Coordinator supremacy and escalation. The moderates are losing ground politically as the war continues.

Lacus Clyne's disappearance following the Junius Seven memorial attack is a political variable. Siegel's enemies could use it against him — evidence of weakness, leverage for concessions. Siegel himself is privately frantic but publicly constrained. He cannot appear desperate without empowering the hardliners.

Lacus — alive, dead, hostage, or free — is leverage for multiple parties. Her status on the Archangel, if known, would reshape the political calculus for every faction. ZAFT moderates would want her recovered safely. ZAFT hardliners might prefer her to remain a useful grievance. The EA might see a bargaining chip. Orb might see a reason to get involved or a reason to stay far away.

#### Entry 12: Spatial Awareness Phenomenon (Newtype)

**Triggers:** spatial awareness, precognitive, sixth sense, instinct beyond, impossible reaction

An undocumented phenomenon observed in rare individuals under sustained high-stress conditions in space environments. Manifests as enhanced spatial cognition, precognitive threat assessment, and in advanced stages, empathic resonance with other individuals exhibiting similar traits. Known unrecognized examples include Mu La Flaga and Rau Le Creuset, both of whom exhibit spatial awareness beyond normal human parameters without any Coordinator enhancement.

No Cosmic Era scientific framework currently accounts for this. Coordinator genetic engineering optimizes for known, measurable parameters — this phenomenon exists outside those parameters entirely. It cannot be engineered because the science that creates Coordinators doesn't know it exists. It represents a natural human evolutionary response to the space environment that predates and exists independently of Coordinator technology.

The political implications, if the phenomenon were studied and publicized, would threaten every faction's ideology simultaneously. Coordinators are not the only path forward for humanity.

~180 tokens.

#### Entry 13a: DRAGOON Systems and Spatial Interface — Current State

**Triggers:** DRAGOON, remote weapon, spatial interface, gunbarrel

DRAGOON (Disconnected Rapid Armament Group Overlook Network) systems are remote weapon platforms guided by the pilot's spatial awareness through a neural-spatial interface. Current implementations are crude — the engineering works but the engineers do not fully understand why, because the underlying spatial awareness phenomenon has never been systematically studied. Only a handful of known pilots can operate DRAGOON-type weapons: Mu La Flaga's wire-guided gunbarrels and Rau Le Creuset's CGUE DRAGOON pods are the most documented examples.

The interface technology is functional but unoptimized. It was built to accommodate an anomaly, not to develop it. The theoretical potential of spatial-responsive mobile suit technology far exceeds current implementation, but no faction has prioritized this development because so few compatible pilots are known to exist.

~140 tokens.

#### Entry 13b: Advanced Spatial-Responsive Technology — Biosensor Through Psychoframe

**Triggers:** psychoframe, biosensor, fin funnels, neural interface, Zeta, Nu Gundam

Advanced spatial-responsive mobile suit technology extends beyond current DRAGOON implementation on a developmental spectrum.

**Biosensor integration (Zeta configuration):** Basic neural-spatial interface embedded in cockpit systems. Suit responsiveness scales with pilot spatial awareness. Under extreme stress or emotional intensity, biosensor can produce performance beyond designed specifications — barrier effects, amplified output, movement exceeding thruster parameters. These peaks are unreliable and not pilot-controlled. Biosensor suits are competitive with advanced conventional suits and can briefly exceed them during peak events.

**Partial psychoframe (Nu Gundam configuration):** Psychoframe material integrated into cockpit and key frame elements. Enables fin funnel remote weapons guided directly by pilot spatial awareness. Consistent amplification of Newtype ability without biosensor unpredictability. Performance scales reliably with pilot capability. Effective against Freedom-class suits. Pilot retains full autonomy. Operational secrecy regarding pilot ability is impossible at this tier — the suit's performance is inexplicable without acknowledging the phenomenon.

Intermediate configurations (ZZ-type, refined biosensor variants) are available as transitional development steps. Canon mobile suit designations are valid configuration references. Technological progression follows narrative conditions, not predetermined schedules.

~220 tokens.

#### Entry 13c: Full Psychoframe and NT-D Systems

**Triggers:** NT-D, Unicorn, full psychoframe, destroy mode, psycommu hijack

Full psychoframe construction embeds Newtype-responsive material throughout the entire mobile suit frame. The primary implementation is the Unicorn configuration featuring the NT-D (Newtype Destroyer) system.

NT-D activates autonomously when pilot Newtype resonance reaches critical threshold, typically triggered by proximity to hostile Newtype-type intent or extreme combat stress. The pilot does not fully control activation or deactivation. During NT-D: enhanced performance across all parameters, psycommu hijack capability seizes control of enemy remote weapons (including DRAGOONs, funnels). NT-D is time-limited. In base NT-D, the suit is competitive with Freedom and Justice class units, with specific advantage against remote weapon loadouts.

At extreme resonance beyond normal NT-D parameters, psychoframe can produce phenomena exceeding mechanical explanation — barrier effects, field repulsion, directed resonance events. These extreme manifestations require conditions approaching the limits of human Newtype capacity and are not reliably reproducible.

Canon mobile suit designations — Unicorn Gundam, Hi-Nu Gundam, and related configurations — are valid references.

~180 tokens.

#### Entry 14: Cosmic Era Mobile Suit Technology — Current State

**Triggers:** Strike systems, phase shift armor, Striker Pack, GAT-X, GINN specifications

Current mobile suit technology in the Cosmic Era is divided by faction. ZAFT fields mass-produced GINN units and the experimental GAT-X series captured at Heliopolis. The Earth Alliance developed the Strike and its sibling units (Duel, Buster, Blitz, Aegis) at Heliopolis before ZAFT's raid.

The Strike features phase-shift armor (energy-dependent physical damage immunity), modular Striker Pack equipment system, and an OS originally designed for Coordinator pilots. Kira Yamato rewrote the OS extensively during his time as pilot — this rewrite remains in the Strike and partially compensates for Natural reaction times, though incompletely.

ZAFT's advanced mobile suit program has developed capabilities beyond current production models — beam weapon miniaturization, improved phase-shift variants, advanced DRAGOON remote weapon integration. Several prototype frames exist in various stages of completion. Who controls access to these prototypes is a political question as much as a military one.

The technological gap between the Strike and ZAFT's next-generation prototypes is significant but not absolute. The Strike's modular equipment system and phase-shift armor remain advantages. The primary limitation is the pilot, not the machine.

#### Entry 15: Junius Seven and the Bloody Valentine

**Triggers:** Junius Seven, Bloody Valentine, memorial, nuclear

The Bloody Valentine tragedy — the nuclear destruction of the PLANT colony Junius Seven — is the defining atrocity of the Cosmic Era conflict. The memorial ceremony near the remains of Junius Seven, where Lacus Clyne was performing, was attacked — the circumstances of that attack and Lacus's subsequent escape pod ejection brought her into the Archangel's path.

Different factions interpret the Bloody Valentine differently. For ZAFT, it is proof that Naturals will resort to genocide. For the EA, it was an escalation they did not officially sanction. For Orb, it is evidence that the war will eventually consume everyone. For the PLANT moderates, it is the wound they are trying to prevent from defining the future. For the hardliners, it is the wound that already has.

Junius Seven is not history. It is a live political weapon that every faction deploys when it serves their purposes.

#### Entry 16: The Archangel — Living Space

**Triggers:** mess hall, observation deck, quarters, corridor, hangar, bunk, off-duty

The Archangel is a warship that people live in. The mess hall serves food that was adequate three weeks ago and is now being stretched. Coffee is always available and always bad. The observation deck on the port side has a viewport facing away from the ship's heading and no chairs — people sit on the floor. Someone's personal music leaks from a bunk at odd hours. The hangar smells like lubricant, ozone, and the chemical edge of phase-shift coolant.

Off-duty hours blur together. People sleep in shifts that don't always align with the lighting cycle. Conversations happen in corridors because there's nowhere private. The ship's hum changes frequency when the engines compensate for something, and the crew has learned to read those changes the way farmers read weather — a shift in pitch means someone on the bridge is worried.

The ship is cramped, overworked, and carrying more people than it was designed for in conditions it wasn't designed for. Every shared space is a potential collision point — who sits where in the mess hall, who makes room in a corridor, whose music is too loud at 0300.

~180 tokens.

### Design Principles

1. **Canon as gravitational field, not rail.** The LLM's knowledge of Gundam SEED is a resource, not a liability. Characters have the same natures. Factions have the same structures. The story lives in the tension between where canon says these people go and where this particular set of pressures actually takes them.

2. **Forces, not trajectories.** Every lorebook entry describes current pressures, decision-making logic, and next likely moves. No entry predicts outcomes or suggests arcs. Gravity's collision system resolves forces into trajectories. That's its job.

3. **The OC's potential is the thematic core.** The Newtype phenomenon is not a minor subplot — it is the question the entire story orbits. What does it mean for humanity if Natural evolution can exceed Coordinator engineering? The card handles the ability with restraint (behavioral detail, not thematic declaration) but the lorebook and the faction logic entries are built so that every faction would be fundamentally disrupted by this revelation. The OC's power progression is the longest arc in the story, and its culmination — in whatever form the collisions produce — is the moment the three-path question gets answered.

4. **Earned power, not granted power.** The OC's progression — OS evolution, Newtype emergence, machine upgrades through the biosensor-to-psychoframe technology tree — is driven by play, not by schedule. The tiered lorebook entries provide technology at each level when the story reaches for it. Canon mobile suit designations (Zeta, Nu Gundam, Unicorn) are explicitly available as configurations.

5. **The filter is off.** The Cosmic Era's themes are portrayed at full weight. Bodies carry the war. Sexuality is present as human behavior under pressure. Institutional violence is specific. Moral complexity is not softened. The anime's sanitizing filter and Kira's plot armor have both been removed.

6. **The secret as slow-burn engine.** The OC's Newtype ability, hidden because of what they've seen happen to the biologically different, is a constraint that Gravity can pressure from every direction across the entire run. Its eventual exposure is the moment that transforms the political landscape of the story.

7. **Asymmetric information everywhere.** Lacus doesn't know about Kira's defection at start. The crew doesn't know about the OC's ability. Kira doesn't know Lacus is on the Archangel. Athrun doesn't know where Lacus is. Le Creuset doesn't know about the OC's phenomenon — yet. The gaps between what different characters know are where the drama lives.

8. **Three-path question stays open.** Nothing in the bundle scripts whether the world burns, returns to canon's trajectory, or finds a third way. The collision structure supports all three. The player's choices, the OC's growth, Lacus's influence, and the faction dynamics determine which attractor wins.

9. **Density over length.** Every field in every card carries weight. No lore dumps. No filler. No style instructions in the cards or lorebook. The preset owns style. The cards own character. The lorebook owns world state. The scenario owns structure. Nothing duplicates.
